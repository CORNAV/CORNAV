Abstract

The construction industry faces persistent labor shortages and one of the highest injury rates among major industries, motivating the adoption of autonomous robots to improve productivity and worker safety. However, existing language-grounded navigation systems primarily rely on semantic scene understanding and lack awareness of construction-specific information such as architectural plans, evolving work schedules, and safety constraints, limiting their ability to reliably localize permanent building features and safely navigate active jobsites. We present CORNAV, a blueprint-grounded, schedule-aware navigation framework for autonomous robots on active construction sites that operate from 2D CAD drawings and operator schedules without requiring a Building Information Model. CORNAV combines hierarchical open-vocabulary scene graphs with registered architectural blueprints to improve object grounding, converts operator schedules into time-varying navigation constraints, and uses a large language model-based safety validation module to escalate hazardous zones before planning. A constraint-aware A* planner then enforces mandatory exclusion zones while preferentially avoiding lower-risk areas. Evaluated in both an indoor office environment and a real construction site, blueprint-based grounding raises task success from 11.1% to 100% over semantic retrieval alone, schedule awareness eliminates all hard-zone violations, and the safety validation module correctly rejects hazardous navigation requests arising from incorrectly labeled operator schedules.
